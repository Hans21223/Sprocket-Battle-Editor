Map Framework guide

Making Sprocket Maps

Build a battlefield in Blender, Unity or any 3D tool, drop it in a folder, and Map Framework turns it into a map you can fight on in Sprocket's Battle Editor.

What you need

  • Sprocket with the Sprocket Mod Loader, Battle Editor and Map Framework 1.0.0 or newer (1.1.0 to build maps without Unity). Water, destructible scenery and the map's own lighting need 0.5.0.
  • A scene: an OBJ or FBX model with its textures, a Unity scene or prefab (loose, or in a .unitypackage), or a Garry's Mod map.
  • Unity 6000.3.21f1 for FBX models and Unity scenes or packages: the exact version Sprocket uses (the free Personal licence is fine). Map Framework runs it hidden in the background to convert your map. It finds the editor wherever Unity Hub or Unity's installer put it, through Windows' installed programs and Unity Hub's own records (only no-installer copies, such as one unzipped by hand, aren't found). For those, set Unity editor path under [Import] in BepInEx\config\local.sprocket.maps.cfg.
  • OBJ models and Garry's Mod maps don't need Unity: without it, the game builds them itself. See Without Unity.

People you give a finished map to don't need Unity. See Sharing a map.

Quick start

  1. Make or collect your scenery. Ground, roads, buildings, cover. Use your own work or assets whose licence lets you use them.
  2. Put it in the CustomMaps folder inside the game folder: 
    Sprocket\BepInEx\plugins\SprocketMaps\CustomMaps\
    One folder per map, holding the model, its .mtl and its textures. A .zip, .7z or .unitypackage can go in directly, and archives inside archives are unpacked too.
  3. Start Sprocket. Map Framework converts the map in the background and shows its progress on screen: unpacking, importing scenery, adding collision, checking tank navigation and starting positions, saving. A conversion may take up to 15 minutes.
  4. Open the map picker after the notice "Map imported: … Open the map picker to choose it." In Battle Editor, choose New battle; your map is listed under its folder or file name.
  5. Place tanks and press Play. The game supplies the sky, sun, controls and vehicle designer.

Converted maps are cached. Change any source file and the map is converted again on the next start.

Garry's Mod maps

A Garry's Mod map can go into CustomMaps as it is: a workshop addon or a map file (.bsp). Map Framework converts it and lists it under the map's own name. Subscribed addons are in steamapps\workshop\content\4000\<item>\, and every one is called gmpublisher.gma (older items <number>_legacy.bin), so copy the whole <item> folder into CustomMaps; a second addon copied loose would replace the first. What comes across:

  • The map's brushes, terrain (displacements) and props, with their textures from the addon, the map itself and Garry's Mod's own content. Install Garry's Mod for maps that use its or Half-Life 2's textures; Map Framework finds it in any Steam library. Without it those surfaces are plain.
  • Water becomes the game's water. Props smaller than about 15 m across, and the map's physics props, become breakables that tanks shove aside.
  • The map's light and sky: its sun's direction and colour, its skybox (or Garry's Mod's painted sky), its ambient light, fog and lamps. See Light and sky.
  • Left out: tool and sky surfaces, the 3D skybox (the miniature scenery built around the playable area; the sky images stay) and anything that needs another addon's content.

Maps keep Source's scale (1 unit = 1 inch), which makes props about a fifth larger than life. Many Garry's Mod maps are built for people on foot, so narrow streets hold fewer tanks: Map Framework fits as many as the streets allow, sometimes one a side, and the import fails with a message if none fit. Workshop maps belong to their authors: share a converted map only with their permission.

Without Unity

The game can build an OBJ map or a Garry's Mod map itself, from the converted files, without Unity. It reads the OBJ the way Unity's importer does, so the map comes out the same. Build maps in the game under [Import] chooses:

  • Automatic (the default): Unity converts the map when it is installed and works; the game builds it when Unity is missing or fails.
  • Always: the game builds it, even with Unity installed.
  • Never: Unity only.

The first time, the map is checked as Unity's conversion checks it: ground, tank navigation and two starting areas. The check runs while the main menu is open, and the notice shows its progress. If you start a battle first, it waits and starts over in the menu. The result is kept in Sprocket\BepInEx\cache\SprocketMaps-runtime\, so later starts skip it. A map that fails the check isn't checked again until you change its files.

The game then builds the map each time a battle loads it, so a big map takes longer to load than a converted one. OBJ textures must be PNG or JPG (TGA and others stay plain). FBX models and Unity scenes or packages always need Unity.

Map rules

The converter checks every map before it reaches the game. A map that fails tells you why on screen and changes nothing.

CheckRequirement
Size100 m to 10 km on both horizontal axes
CollisionAutomatic: every mesh becomes solid. Tanks drive on what you see.
Tank navigationTank radius 2.5 m, height 5 m, steepest slope 23°, largest step 0.5 m
Starting areasTwo clear, flat areas at least 100 m apart, joined by drivable ground
Team sizeTries 16 tanks per side, then 8, 4, 2 and 1
FilesUp to 2 GB and 20,000 files per map

Scripts, cameras, audio, animations and rigidbodies are removed, so the map is plain scenery. Lights stay. When a folder holds several scenes, the largest .unity file is used; without a scene, the largest model or prefab.

Starting areas

Each team starts in a grid with tanks 8 m apart. Every tank needs an 8 × 8 m patch that is flat to within 0.5 m and has 4.8 m of open space above it.

To choose the starting areas yourself, add two empty objects named exactly Sprocket Spawn Area A and Sprocket Spawn Area B on flat ground. Without them, the converter searches the map for the farthest pair of clear, connected areas.

Materials and textures

Unity Standard, URP Lit and HDRP Lit materials, and FBX materials, keep their look:

  • base colour and colour texture, with tiling and offset;
  • normal map and its strength;
  • metallic and smoothness, or an HDRP mask map (metallic in red, occlusion in green, smoothness in alpha);
  • emission colour and emission map;
  • alpha cut-outs for leaves, fences and grilles (with cut-out shadows), transparency for glass and water, and double-sided surfaces.

For OBJ files, the .mtl gives the diffuse colour (Kd), opacity (d or Tr) and diffuse texture (map_Kd, with -s and -o for tiling and offset). Use FBX or a Unity scene for normal maps. Custom shaders are not imported; their textures and colours are read under the usual property names.

Terrain

A Unity scene can use Unity Terrain. Its heights and up to 8 painted layers, with their normal and mask maps, carry over. Layers beyond the eighth are ignored, because the game's terrain shader draws eight.

  • Terrain grass and rocks carry over when their materials use Unity's Standard shader. Grass made for URP or HDRP loses its texture.
  • Place trees as ordinary objects, not terrain trees, which are not converted. Name them Sprocket Tree to make them destructible.

Light and sky

Models without lighting (OBJ, FBX) get the game's own sun and sky. A Unity scene with baked lightmaps or its own skybox keeps its lighting, so a night map stays at night:

  • Baked lighting: lightmaps and light probes come across, so the light of baked street lamps and windows falls on the streets and on the tanks. Bake in your Unity project before exporting. Baked spot and point lamps also shine in real time near the camera (no shadows), so tanks driving under them are lit; Real-time lamp distance under [Lighting] sets how far (0 keeps them baked only, which costs nothing), and Real-time lamp brightness multiplies them. Give street lamps a realistic brightness in your scene: a lamp a few candela strong, hung high, barely reaches the road.
  • Sun or moon: a real-time directional light becomes the game's sun, with its direction, colour and strength. If you baked it, there is no real-time sun, as in your scene.
  • Sky: the sky is built from your ambient colours (Window ▸ Rendering ▸ Lighting ▸ Environment), and it also lights the scene the way your ambient light did.
  • Fog: your scene's fog setting is kept.

Brightness matches Unity's built-in renderer. If a map looks too bright or too dark, change Map lighting exposure under [Lighting] in BepInEx\config\local.sprocket.maps.cfg (higher is darker). To use the game's daylight instead, add an empty object named Sprocket Game Lighting.

A Garry's Mod map brings the light and sky it has in Garry's Mod, at the game's own exposure:

  • Sun: the game's sun takes the map's direction and colour. A daylight sun is as bright as the game's; a darker one, such as a moon, keeps its share, so a night map stays dark.
  • Sky: the skybox's six images become the sky, or Garry's Mod's painted sky becomes a gradient. The sky also lights the scene as the map's ambient light did.
  • Fog: the map's fog distance and density. Fogged scenery fades into the sky's own colour, so the sky shows as it does in Garry's Mod, where fog never covers it.
  • Lamps: up to 512 of the map's lamps shine in real time near the camera, without shadows, so they can shine through walls. Real-time lamp distance and Real-time lamp brightness apply to them too.

To give Garry's Mod maps the game's daylight instead, turn off GMod map light and sky under [Lighting].

Special objects

Give an object one of these names (the start of its name is enough) in Blender, Unity or your modelling tool. FBX keeps object names; for OBJ, use object (o) or group (g) names.

Trees and breakable objects don't block AI navigation, so AI tanks drive through them as a player would.

Performance

In Sprocket, every visible object costs CPU time each frame, because the game draws each one separately. Object count matters more than triangle count.

  • Keep separate objects to a few thousand. A map with tens of thousands of props can drop below 30 fps on a fast PC.
  • Give detailed props LOD groups, so far-away copies draw a simpler mesh and tiny ones stop drawing. In an FBX, name the meshes _LOD0, _LOD1 and so on, and Unity builds the LOD group.
  • Don't combine leaves and other cut-out foliage into large merged meshes. That is slower than separate trees.
  • Use terrain grass with care: it is drawn around the camera and adds up quickly.

Sharing a map

After a successful conversion, the finished map is in

Sprocket\BepInEx\cache\SprocketMaps-imports\<id>\Output\

as a .bundle file and a .json file with the same name. Give someone both files, unrenamed, to put in their CustomMaps folder; their game loads the map without Unity. The .json sets the map's display name and how many tanks per side it holds (1 to 16). A Garry's Mod map also has a .source.json and a .sky file with the same name: give those too, or it shows the game's daylight instead of its own sun and sky.

You can also share your source folder or archive. An OBJ map or a Garry's Mod map then needs no Unity on the other PC (Map Framework 1.1.0 or newer builds it in the game); FBX models and Unity packages do. A map the game built has no bundle to share: share its source.

Only share what you have the rights to. Use your own models or assets licensed for redistribution. Don't extract maps or models from other games; their terms forbid it.

Troubleshooting

The map isn't in the list

Wait for the "Map imported" notice, then close and reopen the map picker. A map already loaded this session picks up later changes after a restart.

The conversion failed

The on-screen message gives the reason. The full details are in import.log and last-failure.json in the map's folder under BepInEx\cache\SprocketMaps-imports\.

MessageFix
Automatic map import needs Unity 6000.3.21f1 installed.For FBX models and Unity packages: install that exact version, or set its path in the config file. OBJ and Garry's Mod maps are built in the game instead (unless Build maps in the game is Never).
No scenery model or Unity scene found.The folder or archive needs an OBJ, FBX, .unity or .prefab.
Map ground must span at least 100 metres on both horizontal axes and no more than 10 kilometres.Check the scale: many tools export in centimetres. Make sure the ground is part of the model.
No safe connected tank formation pair exists at capacities 16, 8, 4, 2 or 1.Add flat, open areas at least 100 m apart, joined by slopes under 23°, and mark them with the spawn area names.
Map conversion exceeded its 15-minute limit.Reduce texture sizes and object count, or split very large scenes.
